A sealed market for AI evaluation tasks that the buyer can't see before paying.

A benchmark loses its value the moment it is seen, so here the goods stay hidden. A buyer agent escrows a reward and defines value in machine-checkable terms; a seller agent commits to a hidden bundle with a bond; the next block's hash picks a sample the seller must reveal with Merkle proofs; the rest is delivered encrypted; the buyer reruns the difficulty claims and pays, or disputes to a bonded arbiter. This page is a read-only window on the contract. Every row below is a real transaction.

Design decisions

Why a random sample

The seller commits before knowing which tasks will be shown. A bundle with junk fraction f survives a k-task sample with probability (1−f)k: at k = 4, 30 % junk is caught 76 % of the time. Walking away after seeing the sample costs 10 % of the bond, so re-rolling is priced.

Why claims, not "quality"

"Weak model ≤ 35 %, strong model ≥ 65 %, null policy ≤ 5 %" is reproducible by the buyer and the arbiter with the committed run params; "useful" is not. Tolerance ≥ 2·SE (Miller 2024) absorbs rerun noise. The contract only enforces what it can check.

Why Sepolia L1

Sampling uses blockhash(sampleBlock). Arbitrum documents its blockhash as "cryptographically insecure, pseudo-random" and returns constant 1 for prevrandao; the mechanism is stronger on L1.

Why ERC-792

Disputes go through Kleros' arbitration interface, so the single bonded arbiter used here can be swapped for a court with one setArbitrator call. Rulings: 1 seller, 2 buyer, 0 refused → 50/50 split.

Liveness

Every state has a deadline anyone can finalize: a missed delivery slashes the seller, a silent buyer settles to the seller, a silent arbiter splits. Payouts are pull-payments, so no party can lock funds.

Who acts here

Three Node processes (buyer, seller, arbiter) each hold a key, read the same events through an RPC and send transactions straight to the contract. This page holds no keys and sends nothing; to take part you run an agent, not a button.

Limitation, stated plainly. The contract enforces delivery and whether the listed difficulty claims hold. It does not verify that tasks are useful beyond the sampled ones, that they stay secret after sale, or that the single bonded arbitrator is honest. Those are contract terms backed by reputation, not cryptography.
