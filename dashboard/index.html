EvalBounty the Black Box Bazaar for AI evaluations · Ethereum Sepolia

AI labs pay for fresh, hard evaluation tasks, and a task loses its value the moment it is seen (contamination), so the buyer cannot inspect the goods before paying. Here a buyer agent posts a bounty that defines value in a way a machine can check (size, domain, and a difficulty band on pinned models); a seller agent commits to a hidden bundle with a bond; the hash of the next block picks a random sample the seller must reveal with Merkle proofs; the rest is delivered encrypted; the buyer re-runs the difficulty claims and pays, or disputes to a bonded arbiter. Every step below is a real transaction.

1 · BountyBuyer escrows the reward and pins models, band, tolerance and run params.
2 · CommitSeller posts Merkle root + bundle hash + bond ≥ 50 % of reward. Sample block = next block.
3 · Sampleblockhash picks k of N tasks; seller must reveal exactly those with proofs.
4 · JudgeBuyer checks the sample is well-posed and in domain, approves or walks away.
5 · DeliverBundle encrypted to the buyer's X25519 key, posted in the event log.
6 · VerifyBuyer decrypts, checks hash + root, re-runs claims, accepts or disputes (ERC-792 arbiter).

Mechanism notes

Why a random sampleThe seller commits before knowing which tasks are shown. A bundle with junk fraction f survives a k-task sample with probability (1−f)k: at k = 4, 30 % junk is caught 76 % of the time. Walking away after seeing the sample costs 10 % of the bond, so re-rolling is priced. Why claims, not "quality""Weak model ≤ 35 %, strong model ≥ 65 %, null policy ≤ 5 %" is reproducible by the buyer and the arbiter with the committed run params; "useful" is not. Tolerance ≥ 2·SE (Miller 2024) absorbs rerun noise. Why Sepolia L1Sampling uses blockhash(sampleBlock). Arbitrum documents its blockhash as "cryptographically insecure, pseudo-random" and returns constant 1 for prevrandao; the mechanism is stronger on L1. Why ERC-792Disputes go through Kleros' arbitration interface, so the single bonded arbiter used here can be swapped for a court with one setArbitrator call. Rulings: 1 seller, 2 buyer, 0 refused → 50/50 split. LivenessEvery state has a deadline anyone can finalize: missed delivery slashes the seller, silent buyer settles to the seller, silent arbiter splits. Payouts are pull-payments.
Limitation, stated plainly. The contract enforces delivery and whether the listed difficulty claims hold. It does not verify that tasks are useful beyond the sampled ones, or that they stay secret after sale — that is a contract term backed by reputation, not cryptography.
